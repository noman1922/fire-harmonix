MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATIONS & RESEARCH PROTOCOL

Scientific Methodology Dossier

A transparent exposition of the cross-sensor calibration problem, standardization equations, spatial indexing, index formulation, and machine-learning feasibility benchmarks.

SECTION 01

The Multi-Sensor Calibration Challenge

Spaceborne active fire monitoring fundamentally relies on detecting sub-pixel thermal infrared radiance anomalies against cooler ambient backgrounds. However, observations gathered from distinct orbital instruments cannot be directly aggregated without introducing substantial distortion:

  • Spatial Resolution: MODIS pixels measure ~1,000 m at nadir, whereas VIIRS delivers ~375 m resolution (a 7:1 pixel area difference). A single fire front that registers as one MODIS detection may trigger multiple adjacent VIIRS detections.
  • Sensitivity & Saturation: VIIRS I-band sensors detect low-intensity, smoldering sub-pixel fires that escape MODIS detection thresholds. Conversely, MODIS instruments carry higher saturation ceilings for intense thermal events.
  • Orbital Timing: Instruments operate on differing sun-synchronous orbital tracks (Terra: ~10:30 AM/PM; Aqua and Suomi-NPP: ~01:30 PM/AM), capturing different phases of the diurnal burn cycle.
SECTION 02

Mathematical Formulations

1. Within-Sensor Z-Score Normalization
z_{i,s} = \frac{x_{i,s} - \mu_s}{\sigma_s}

Brightness and Fire Radiative Power (FRP) are normalized strictly within each sensor cohort $s \in \{\text{MODIS}, \text{VIIRS}\}$. This prevents models from mistaking sensor resolution for fire intensity.

2. Common Spatial Grid Discretization (0.05°)
g_{lat} = \lfloor latitude / 0.05 \rfloor, \quad g_{lon} = \lfloor longitude / 0.05 \rfloor

Continuous geographic coordinates are grouped into consistent spatial grid cells (~5.5 km resolution), providing a unified spatial indexing surface.

3. Sensor Agreement Metric
Agreement = 1 - \frac{|M - V|}{|M| + |V| + \epsilon}

Calculates relative consistency between normalized sensor readings in co-located grid cells, yielding a bounded agreement coefficient between 0 and 1.

4. Harmonized Fire Activity Index (HFAI)
HFAI = 0.25\,C + 0.25\,F + 0.20\,B + 0.15\,A + 0.15\,Q

Where $C$ represents detection density, $F$ represents normalized FRP, $B$ is normalized brightness, $A$ is the sensor agreement score, and $Q$ is sensor confidence quality.

SECTION 03

Machine Learning Feasibility Benchmarks

In addition to the deterministic dashboard, the repository includes an offline feasibility study located under ml/train_models.py:

Isolation Forest (Anomaly Isolation)

Trained unsupervised on multi-feature grid-day arrays (detection volume, FRP quantiles, brightness variance, day/night ratios) to identify statistical outliers without requiring unverified ground truth wildfire labels.

XGBoost Regressor (24h Activity Forecast)

Estimates next-day mean HFAI based on 7-day and 14-day rolling activity features. Evaluated using strict chronological train/validation/test splits.

CRITICAL BOUNDARY

Scientific & Operational Boundaries

Thermal anomaly $\neq$ confirmed wildfire: A satellite thermal detection indicates an infrared radiance threshold exceedance. Industrial flaring, prescribed agricultural burns, volcanoes, and surface glare can trigger anomalies.

Engineering metric $\neq$ emergency warning: The Harmonized Fire Activity Index (HFAI) is a calibrated research construct designed for cross-sensor scientific alignment. It is not an official public safety alert or civil defense product.
